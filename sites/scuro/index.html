The approach

Driven first.Written about after.

Nothing here is parked for show. Each car is driven, photographed and then put into words, in that order.

PorscheFerrariMcLarenAudi Sport

Marques in the garage: Porsche, Ferrari, McLaren and Audi Sport.

Cohesion

A car feels right when design, air and mechanics agree.

01 / 03

Design

Proportion comes first. A low nose, a cabin set between the axles and surfaces that carry light from one end to the other.

02 / 03

Aerodynamics

On the 911 GT3 RS the wing stands above the roof line and the car makes 860 kg of downforce at 285 km/h. Air is treated as a material.

03 / 03

Engineering

Forged wheels and the brakes behind them. The parts that rarely get photographed decide how a car feels at the limit.

A car should say something before it moves. Every line, every material and every sound is a decision someone made on purpose.

Vehicles

Seven cars. Each one kept for a single reason, stated plainly.

    Interior

    The cabin is where a car is actually experienced. These are the surfaces that matter.

    01 / 04

    The wheel

    The first thing a car says to its driver comes through the rim: its thickness, its material, the way it turns in the hand.

    02 / 04

    The seat

    A carbon shell holds the body low and still, with the model name stitched where the shoulders rest.

    03 / 04

    The console

    Everything the driver touches sits on one slim spine of carbon. Red marks the controls that change the car.

    04 / 04

    The materials

    Perforated leather on a flat-bottomed rim, with the paddles right behind it. Daily use, taken seriously.

    Exhaust

    Every engine has a voice. It comes from the layout, the firing order and how high it is allowed to rev.

    Maximum engine speed, rpm

    Porsche 911 GT39,000rpm

    Naturally aspirated flat-six. A hard-edged howl that keeps climbing past the point where most engines have stopped.

    Ferrari 296 GTB8,500rpm

    Twin-turbo V6 with a 120° angle between the banks. During development, Ferrari's engineers nicknamed it the piccolo V12.

    Ferrari F8 Tributo8,000rpm

    Twin-turbo V8 on a flat-plane crankshaft, the reason for its quick, high bark.

    About

    Scuro means dark in Italian. It is a private garage imagined as a publication: few cars, strong opinions, careful photography.

    SCURO is a concept, designed and built by GuDev as a study in automotive web design. It is not affiliated with Porsche, Ferrari, McLaren or Audi, and the cars shown are not for sale here.
